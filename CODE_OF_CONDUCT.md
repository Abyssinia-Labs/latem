# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to get something to work.

## What that means here

This is a small project about a narrow problem, and most of what passes through
it will be bug reports, patches and disagreements about design. So:

- **Argue about the work, not the person.** "This breaks INV-3 because a ref
  reaches a shell" is useful. "This is careless" is not, and the first sentence
  says everything the second was reaching for.
- **A report is a gift, including a blunt one.** Somebody took the time to tell
  you your software is broken. The acceptance tests in `docs/` exist because
  that turned out to be the most valuable thing anyone did for this project.
- **Being right is not a licence.** Correct and contemptuous is still
  unwelcome, and it costs the project people it needs.
- **Nobody owes you an answer.** Maintainers here are few, and an unanswered
  issue is usually time rather than a judgement about you.

Harassment, personal attacks, and any of the usual bigotries are not welcome,
and that is not a matter of tone. This applies to issues, pull requests, commit
messages and anywhere else this project is the subject.

## If something goes wrong

Report it to the maintainers privately, through GitHub — open a private security
advisory if you need a confidential channel and there is no better one, or
contact the owner of this repository directly.

Maintainers will edit, hide or delete contributions that do not belong here, and
will say why when saying why is useful. Repeated or deliberate behaviour gets a
ban, without a debate about whether it was severe enough.

Maintainers are held to this too. A maintainer who breaks it should expect to
hear about it from the others, and from you.
